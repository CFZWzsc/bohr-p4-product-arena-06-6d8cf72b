# Demo paper (synthetic)

## Abstract

We investigate whether a porous carbon electrode can retain capacitance under long cycling.

We report a 92 % retention after 5000 cycles at 1 A/g.

## 1. Introduction

Supercapacitors require electrodes that keep their capacitance over repeated charging.

The research question is whether a resorcinol-derived porous carbon electrode is stable over 5000 cycles.

## 2. Methods

The electrode was prepared from resorcinol and formaldehyde and tested in 1 M H2SO4 at 1 A/g.

Three identical cells were measured; no statistical dispersion is reported.

## 3. Results

The electrode reached 210 F/g at 1 A/g.

After 5000 cycles it retained 92 % of its initial capacitance.

## 4. Discussion

The retention is consistent with a stable pore structure.

A limitation is that the specific capacitance at 5 A/g was not measured, so rate capability is unknown.

## 5. Conclusion

The resorcinol-derived porous carbon electrode is stable over 5000 cycles at 1 A/g.
